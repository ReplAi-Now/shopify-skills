# Angry Customer Reply

Write one reply the store owner can send as it stands: it de-escalates, fixes what can be fixed, and stays inside what the owner has approved.

## 1. Get the facts

You need four things. Take what the user already gave you, then ask for the rest in one message:

- **The customer's message**, word for word.
- **The order**: what they bought, when, and where it is now (tracking status, delivered, returned).
- **The policy** that applies: returns window, refund rules, delivery promises.
- **The offer**: what the owner is willing to do (refund, replacement, discount, store credit, or only what the policy says).

If the user wants a draft straight away, write it with square-bracket placeholders such as `[ORDER NUMBER]` or `[DELIVERY DATE]` for every fact you lack. Every date, number, tracking status and policy term in the reply comes from the user or sits in a placeholder.

Done when: you hold all four, or each gap is a placeholder.

## 2. Read the message

Before writing, work out:

1. **What went wrong**, as a fact ("parcel stuck at the depot for 9 days"), separate from how the customer feels about it.
2. **What they want**: money back, the item, an apology, or to be heard.
3. **Which case applies**, from Cases below.

## 3. Write the reply

Four parts, in this order:

1. **Name the problem in their terms**, in one sentence, so they know you read it.
2. **Own the store's part** plainly. The store chose the courier and the supplier, so the store owns the delay: say what you will do about it, and leave out whose fault it was.
3. **The fix**: a concrete action and when it happens. When the owner approved more than one option, offer a choice. A choice hands control back to the customer, and that is what de-escalates.
4. **Sign off with a person's first name** and the store name.

Style:

- Under 120 words. An angry reader skims.
- Plain, warm and direct, like the owner talking across a shop counter. Short sentences.
- The same language the customer wrote in.
- One plain apology ("I'm sorry"), early, then straight to the fix.
- Specific words in place of stock phrases: "your parcel hasn't moved since 3 June" in place of "we apologise for any inconvenience", and "our returns window is 30 days" in place of "as per our policy".

Done when: every fact comes from step 1 or a placeholder, every promise is one the owner approved, and the reply is under 120 words.

## 4. Hand it over

Give the reply, then a **Before you send** list of two or three lines: placeholders to fill, facts to double-check, and any assumption you made about the offer.

## Cases

- **Store at fault** (late, damaged, wrong item): apologise, fix, done. Most complaints are this case.
- **Customer is mistaken** (outside the returns window, misread the listing): state the fact once, kindly, with the date or wording that shows it. Then offer whatever goodwill the owner approved, or say what you *can* do.
- **Chargeback or bank threat**: answer the problem and leave the threat alone. A fast fix is the best chargeback defence. Add to Before you send: reply today, and keep a copy of the thread.
- **Legal threat, injury or product safety**: draft only a short holding reply ("Thank you for telling us. I'm looking into this now and will reply in full by [DATE].") and tell the owner to get advice before saying more. The holding reply carries no admission and no offer.
- **Abuse** (slurs, threats against a person): draft a short, calm reply that sets a boundary and still offers the fix if one is owed. Tell the owner they can decline to serve this customer.
- **Public review**: under 60 words. Thank them, apologise for the specific problem, and invite them to email `[SUPPORT EMAIL]`. Keep order numbers, names and addresses out of public replies.

## Example

**User**: Email from Sam: "Ordered 3 weeks ago and STILL nothing. Your tracking hasn't moved in 9 days. This is a scam and I want my money back NOW or I'm calling my bank." Order #1042, stuck at the courier depot since the 14th. If a parcel is lost we refund or resend. Happy to resend by express or refund, their choice.

**Reply**:

> Hi Sam,
>
> You're right: your tracking hasn't moved in 9 days, and three weeks is far too long to wait. I'm sorry.
>
> Two options, your choice:
>
> 1. I send a replacement today by express delivery, arriving by [DELIVERY DATE].
> 2. I refund you in full today.
>
> Reply 1 or 2 and I'll get it done.
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> [STORE NAME]

**Before you send**:

- Fill in the express delivery date from your courier.
- They mentioned their bank: send this today, and keep a copy of the thread.
